# 2026-10-18: Day 0, the idea and the research

> My mum says *kara do* every time her phone changes, and I'm always happy to help. This project is so that one day she's the one saying "come, let me show you".

**The problem.** Our parents learn to change the phone's brightness once. Then an update moves the option and they're stuck again. The same thing happens in YouTube, Spotify, WhatsApp and food-delivery apps, which change even faster. Right now the "help system" is a son or daughter on a video call, sometimes from another country.

**What happened today**
- I brainstormed problem areas (safety, accessibility, daily life) and picked this one.
- I ran a research sweep with several AI models to see what already exists ([research](../research/existing-tools.md)).
- I made four early decisions: [ADR-0001](../decisions/0001-live-screen-guidance.md), [ADR-0002](../decisions/0002-android-first.md), [ADR-0003](../decisions/0003-point-dont-do.md) and [ADR-0004](../decisions/0004-on-device-first.md).

**Key finding.** No product combines live screen reading, pointing at the exact button, and Hindi on phones. Gemini Live talks about your screen but doesn't point. Microsoft Copilot points, but only on Windows. Small Android apps that point rely on fixed, hand-written guides.

**Biggest risks**
- Trust: scammers abuse the same screen-reading permission.
- Discovery: elders don't search app stores.
- Big tech getting good at simply *doing* tasks for people.

**Naming.** The working name was "Raah", then "Karva Do", and finally **Kara Do (करा दो)**: what parents say when they want help getting something done on the phone.

**Later the same night (19 Oct)**
- Wrote system design v0.1 and picked the 10 MVP tasks.
- Decided: no internet permission in the MVP ([ADR-0005](../decisions/0005-no-internet-mvp.md)), community-contributed recipes ([ADR-0006](../decisions/0006-community-recipes.md), proposed), and setup only with a trusted helper ([ADR-0007](../decisions/0007-trusted-helper-setup.md)).
- Language rule: everyday Hinglish, with tech words such as Bluetooth and scroll kept in English letters.
